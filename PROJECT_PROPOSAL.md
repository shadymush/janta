## ABSTRACT
Kenya’s unemployment rate stands at 6.6% (KNBS, 2023), with youth unemployment exceeding 35%, despite 89% internet penetration and 65% smartphone adoption (Communications Authority of Kenya, 2023). This paradox underscores systemic constraints in employment acquisition, including fragmented job platforms, high recruitment costs, and skill mismatches. Janta, a proposed web application, addresses these challenges by integrating job listings and freelance opportunities into a single AI-driven platform. The system will leverage Kenya’s growing digital infrastructure to provide recruiters with affordable job postings and job seekers with tailored opportunities. Key innovations include a tiered pricing model for recruiters, AI-powered skill-to-job matching, and a freelance bidding module with secure mobile payment integration (M-Pesa). The project will adopt Agile methodology to ensure iterative development and stakeholder feedback. Expected outcomes include reduced recruitment costs by 40%, improved job matching efficiency, and increased access to gig economy opportunities for freelancers. By addressing digital literacy gaps through user-friendly design and localized content, Janta aims to empower Kenya’s workforce despite persistent barriers like high data costs and uneven digital literacy. This study highlights the urgent need for context-specific technological solutions to unlock Kenya’s employment potential.

## CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 Background to the Study
Kenya’s job market is characterized by a stark contradiction: robust internet connectivity (43.3 million users) coexists with chronic underemployment, particularly among youth and women. While platforms like LinkedIn and Brighter Monday exist, they cater largely to formal sectors, excluding 83% of Kenya’s workforce engaged in informal and gig economies (World Bank, 2022). Additionally, 62% of job seekers lack digital literacy to navigate existing platforms effectively (TIFA Research, 2023). Recruiters face prohibitive costs (up to Ksh 50,000 per job post on LinkedIn), limiting opportunities for SMEs. **Janta** emerges as a response to these constraints, leveraging Kenya’s 65% smartphone adoption rate to democratize access to employment through a simplified, mobile-first web interface.

### 1.2 Statement of the Problem
Despite Kenya’s digital advancements, four critical barriers hinder employment acquisition:
1. **Platform Fragmentation:** Job seekers juggle multiple platforms (e.g., BrighterMonday for formal jobs, Upwork for freelancing), increasing search costs.
2. **Recruiter Costs:** High fees exclude SMEs, which contribute 80% of Kenya’s jobs.
3. **Skill Mismatch:** 54% of employers report mismatches between job requirements and applicant skills (KEPSA, 2023).
4. **Digital Exclusion:** Limited vernacular support and high data costs (Ksh 50/GB) marginalize rural users.

### 1.3 Objectives
**General Objective:**
To develop a unified web platform that mitigates employment barriers in Kenya through affordable recruitment solutions and AI-driven skill matching.

**Specific Objectives:**
1. To design a low-cost job posting system with tiered pricing (Ksh 500–5,000).
2. To integrate an AI algorithm that matches skills with job requirements using NLP.
3. To create a freelance module with M-Pesa payment integration for gig workers.

### 1.4 Significance of the Study
- **Job Seekers:** Reduced search costs and improved access to informal/gig opportunities.
- **Recruiters:** Affordable outreach to a broader talent pool, including rural areas.
- **Policy Makers:** Insights into leveraging digital tools for employment creation.

### 1.5 Scope of the Study
The platform will focus on:
- **Users:** Kenyan job seekers, freelancers, and SMEs.
- **Technology:** Web-based (no mobile app) with USSD fallback for low-internet users.
- **Exclusions:** International job markets and advanced AI features like video analytics.

### 1.6 Assumptions
1. Users have basic smartphone literacy.
2. Recruiters prioritize cost-effective hiring solutions.

### 1.7 Limitations and Delimitations
- **Limitations:** Reliance on internet connectivity excludes offline populations.
- **Delimitations:** Focus on Kenya’s urban and peri-urban regions (85% of internet users).

**Key Constraints Highlighted:**
1. **High Data Costs:** Average Ksh 50/GB limits platform accessibility.
2. **Digital Literacy Gaps:** 62% of users struggle with complex UI/UX.
3. **SME Exclusion:** 70% of SMEs cannot afford existing recruitment platforms.
